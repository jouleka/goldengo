## The Count — your wallet as a place that can tell the truth

**Pitch:** Your wallet becomes a real place in Goldengo, fed automatically by the ATM withdrawals statement import already sees plus cash income, drained by every cash-pinned spend. Whenever you feel like it — never on a schedule, never prompted — you open The Count and tap the notes actually in your hand: two 1000s, a 500, three 100-lek coins. Goldengo compares counted to expected and offers the gap as one quiet ghost: '1,400 lekë slipped by since Tuesday — keep it as street money?' One tap and the books are true again. Between counts the engine runs anyway: each withdrawal drains FIFO like an income tranche, and the unaccounted remainder reads as fog in physical notes — 'about a 2000 and two 500s' — with a one-tap sweep. Over months it learns your withdrawal half-life: 'a 10,000 usually lasts you 8 days; likely ATM run Thursday.' The app's standing line: cash moves; you don't owe it a story.

**Why novel:** Every shipped tracker treats an ATM withdrawal as a terminal expense called 'Cash' and then goes blind; the cash 'accounts' in Spendee/Wallet/Toshl/Money Manager are a balance-edit field buried in settings — a shameful silent correction, an admission of failure. Nobody joins statement-imported ATM lines against hand-logged cash spends to derive the gap automatically, nobody counts a wallet at denomination level, nobody computes a personal withdrawal decay curve, and nobody has shipped 'permission to not remember' — every competitor's implicit contract is total recall. It is only possible because Goldengo holds both halves (the statement side and the granular cash side) in one trusted on-device store, which no aggregator-dependent app can do in Albania.

**Why useful:** The single biggest data hole in a cash-heavy life is between 'withdrew 10,000' and 'where did it go.' A ten-second count repairs a week of missed logs without interrogating your memory; the fog line answers the daily glance 'does my pocket match the app'; the half-life says hit the ATM before the weekend. It converts the most anxious moment in tracking — where did it go? — into the calmest, and it is what finally lets the Weight trust the cash balance.

**Fit:** Composes directly with Provenance: the wallet is a funding source, so FIFO and funded-by chips extend to cash with zero new concepts. Drift and sweep are the hard-won never-silently-fabricate principle applied to the messiest data in the app — always a one-tap ghost, never a hidden adjustment. Counting is optional and unscheduled (no homework, no reminders, no red), drift is weather rather than failure, and notes-denominated display makes the money felt, not scored.

**Build sketch:** Cycle 1: tag ATM rows during import — keyword patterns including 'terheqje' in /Sources/GoldengoImport/StatementRowMapper.swift and RaiffeisenAlbaniaParser.swift; CashWallet source kind plus CashTranche (amount, date, remaining) drained FIFO, mirroring the ProvenanceAllocator API shape so its tests carry over; WalletCount @Model holding a DenominationTally keyed on a data-driven Bank of Albania table — notes 200/500/1000/2000/5000/10000 (the 10,000 note verified against bankofalbania.org), coins 5/10/20/50/100, parallel EUR/USD tables; PocketCheck SwiftUI grid with oversized tap-to-increment targets and the shipped drop haptic; drift/sweep ghosts reuse the Rhythm/Due ghost-row component, and an accepted ghost becomes a real ExpenseRecord with a reserved drift kind flowing through existing FIFO allocation. Cycle 2: half-life as median days-to-drain per withdrawal-size bucket (percentile math, no ML); greedy note decomposition over [10000,5000,2000,1000,500,200] for the fog renderer; Home card and widget line via SharedSummary. All Decimal math in memory post-fetch, never in a #Predicate (known SIGSEGV). Fully deterministic, fully offline.

**Curator:** Merged from five overlapping cash-reconciliation concepts — The Count (wallet-as-till frame, denomination table), The Drift ('permission to not remember' and the consentful drift ghost, the best soul framing of the cluster), Cash Fog (withdrawal FIFO tranches, fog-rendered-as-notes, half-life), The Wad (wad-lifespan reading), and the Wallet Truth seed. Cash Census's camera note-counting was discarded: a Create ML banknote classifier is a cycle of risk to save four taps that the stepper grid already makes pleasant. This is the anchor of the shortlist; Kusur Keypad shares its denomination substrate.

**Prior-art verdict:** VERDICT: survives — but the whyNovel overclaims in one place and should be reworded before it ships as a pitch.

What I tried to kill it with (closest prior art found):

1. YNAB cash-account workflow (documented, first-class): an ATM withdrawal categorized as "Transfer: Cash" (auto-applied via payee rules after first setup) feeds a Cash tracking account; hand-logged cash spends drain it; Reconciliation asks "what's your current balance?" and creates an explicit, visible "Reconciliation Balance Adjustment" transaction for the gap. This is the same arithmetic as count-vs-expected-with-sweep, at total level. The concept's claim that competitors only offer "a shameful silent correction buried in settings" is dishonest as applied to YNAB — reconciliation there is a celebrated, explicit flow, not hidden. Toshl likewise auto-creates a named "reconciliation entry" when you edit an account balance (confirmed on toshl.com docs), and Money Manager/Bluecoins/Wallet-by-BudgetBakers create visible adjustment records too. So "nobody derives the gap and records it openly" is FALSE and must not be the novelty hook.

2. Auto-fed cash wallet from detected withdrawals: Walnut/axio (India) auto-detected ATM withdrawals from bank SMS and let users log cash spends against them with double-count protection; Zen Money (CIS) does similar SMS-driven cash-account crediting. 22seven (South Africa) lets users split imported ATM lines into cash categories. So "statement-side feeding the cash ledger" exists in spirit, just via SMS/aggregator instead of statement import.

3. Denomination counting: ubiquitous as standalone utilities (Money Counter, Cash Calculator, Tilly Tally) and as till-reconciliation in POS software (Square/Lightspeed/Loyverse end-of-day drawer counts record over/short at denomination level — literally the wallet-as-till mechanic, in a different product category for a different job).

What I could NOT kill, despite targeted searches (including Japanese-language kakeibo/wallet-app searches, since Japan is the most likely place this would exist): no shipped personal expense tracker, mainstream or indie, any platform, that (a) counts the wallet at denomination level inside the tracker and reconciles it against a ledger-derived expected balance; (b) renders the unaccounted remainder as physical notes ("about a 2000 and two 500s"); (c) computes a personal withdrawal half-life / predicts the next ATM run (consumer-facing — only B2B ATM-network forecasting exists, e.g. Brinks/Loomis); or (d) frames the gap as a consentful one-tap ghost rather than a neutral adjustment. Japanese wallet-balance apps (現金管理, 財布くん, Money Forward's 財布 accounts) track wallet totals only — confirmed no denomination entry on the 現金管理 App Store listing.

Net: the gap-derivation claim is covered by prior art (YNAB/Toshl) and the auto-fed-wallet claim is partially covered (Walnut/Zen Money); but the core composed mechanic — denomination-native count, fog-as-notes, withdrawal half-life, ghost-not-adjustment — has no nameable prior art in the tracker space, and the composition is the product. Survives, conditional on rewriting whyNovel to drop "nobody derives the gap automatically" and "silent shameful correction" (false for YNAB/Toshl) and lead instead with denomination-level counting, fog-in-notes, half-life, and the no-aggregator statement-import substrate, which are the genuinely unclaimed parts.

**Usefulness verdict:** Tried hard to kill it; it doesn't die, because its value does not depend on the ritual. The honest tap accounting: the engine's expected-wallet balance is a free byproduct of two shipped behaviors — statement import already sees ATM rows (RaiffeisenAlbaniaParser.swift exists; tagging 'terheqje' is a keyword filter) and cash-pinned spends already flow through the gol-89/90 Paid-from picker. Zero new daily taps. Even a user who NEVER opens the count grid gets the fog line and the one-tap sweep, so the week-two question ('will he still count?') is the wrong kill shot — counting is the optional repair tool, not the engagement loop. The recurring pain is real and structural, not aesthetic: in a no-aggregator, cash-heavy life, the gap between 'withdrew 10,000' and logged spends re-opens every single week forever, and today the only options are logging every byrek (high friction, guaranteed to lapse) or a silent balance edit (which poisons the Weight and Provenance for the cash half of the user's life — i.e., most of it). A 10-second count that batch-repairs a week of missed logs is a friction REDUCER relative to the status quo, and the drift-ghost keeps monthly totals true instead of hiding the correction. The intrinsic recount trigger is also sound: the daily 'does my pocket match the app' glance going visibly stale is a concrete, self-renewing reason to count — no streaks or nags needed. Where the gimmick critique DOES land, on the flourishes: (1) denomination-level tallying over a plain total is defensible (tapping notes as you flip through them is arguably faster than mental addition + typing) but the EUR/USD parallel tables and coin granularity are speculative scope — a count of a foreign-note stash is a once-a-quarter event at best; ship lek-only first. (2) The half-life predictor is statistically thin for months — a single user produces maybe 3-6 withdrawals/month, so median-per-size-bucket needs a quarter of data before 'likely ATM run Thursday' is anything but noise; correctly staged as cycle 2, and its failure mode is silence, not wrongness. (3) Fog-rendered-as-notes is presentation, charming but trimmable. None of these flourishes is load-bearing; the core (auto FIFO wallet + drift ghost + sweep) survives without them. Verdict: not a demo — it is the missing reconciliation layer that makes every already-shipped feature (Weight, Provenance, funded-by chips) truthful for the dominant payment medium in the user's actual life, at near-zero recurring cost. Survives, with the recommendation to cut EUR/USD denomination tables from cycle 1 and treat half-life as a bonus, not a promise.

## Kusur Keypad — log by the notes you handed over

**Pitch:** At the pazar you don't know you spent 350 lekë — you know you handed a 500 and got a 100 and a 50 back. So that's how you log it: tap the note you gave, tap the change you received, done — the amount computes itself while the gold 500 slides out of your wallet and two small notes slide back in. It's faster than typing digits and it feels exactly like the purchase did. An optional 'pazar session' pill groups a burst of these into one named outing — the eight-stall market run no app can currently capture without misery.

**Why novel:** Every keypad in Mint/YNAB/Spendee/Toshl and the entire indie scene assumes you know the price as an abstract number — a card-economy assumption. In a no-minor-unit cash economy the physical exchange of notes IS the primary memory of the transaction; the price is derived. No tracker on any platform has an entry method based on the handover of notes. This inverts the keypad itself.

**Why useful:** Lower-tap than a numpad for most real cash prices (one or two note taps plus change beats typing three or four digits and confirming), workable one-handed while holding shopping bags, and more accurate — people misremember prices but remember which note they broke. Every entry also updates The Count's denomination inventory for free, so reconciliation drift shrinks the more naturally you log. It makes the single most frequent action in a cash life — the small hand-to-hand payment — nearly frictionless.

**Fit:** The Weight made literal: spending depicted as the physical act it was, with a per-note drop haptic — felt, never scored. Low-tap is the design soul's first commandment and this beats the numpad on taps. A Rhythm Ledger ghost usual can open pre-set to the note you always break for it; sessions are a grouping label, not a mode with rules — nothing assumed, no homework.

**Build sketch:** One cycle on top of The Count's denomination table, or standalone with inventory updates disabled. A custom SwiftUI keypad of note/coin chips in two rows (handed / received); amount = sum(handed) − sum(received), validated non-negative; writes a normal Expense plus an optional ExchangeDetail (denominations in/out) and adjusts the wallet tally when The Count is enabled. Pazar session is a nullable outingID on Expense with a start/stop pill in the Add tab — no scheduling, no state machine. One matchedGeometryEffect animation of notes leaving the wallet stack, CoreHaptics per note. An App Intent variant takes two denomination lists for Shortcuts and voice. All deterministic code, no model calls.

**Curator:** Survived dedupe intact — nothing else in the batch covers entry. Kept deliberately paired with The Count (shared denomination model) while standalone-capable. Pound for pound the most 'only buildable here' idea on the list: it exists because the lek has no minor unit.

**Prior-art verdict:** VERDICT: Survives. I hunted in the niches where this would live — Japanese cash-culture apps (searched in Japanese), Indian denomination cashbooks, POS/cashier tools, open-source, tabletop coin trackers — and found plenty of adjacent art but nothing covering the core mechanic: a personal expense tracker whose ENTRY METHOD is 'tap notes handed + tap change received, amount = difference, expense logged.'

Nearest prior art found, and why each fails to kill:
1. Denomination calculators/counters — i金種計算機 (iOS JP), Indian Cash Denomination Calculator, 'Cash Calculator & Credit Debit' (Play Store), and 'Cash Book: Expense Tracker' (India) which bundles a Cash Counter alongside its ledger. These tap denominations to COUNT a total you possess; none derive a purchase price from handed-minus-received, and the counter is a side tool, not the expense keypad.
2. POS cash-tender flows — Square, SwipeSimple, every register: tap tendered quick-amount, system computes change. This is the INVERSE derivation (price known → change derived), merchant-side, and doesn't log the payer's expense.
3. Japanese wallet-balance apps — 現金管理 (App Store JP), Zaim/MoneyForward ME 'wallet' accounts, 財布くん: track cash balance, but entry is a normal numeric amount; no denomination handover.
4. おつり貯金 / round-up savings (Toranoko, finbee, JP) — compute virtual change FROM an entered amount given a chosen note; again the inverse derivation, and a savings gimmick, not an entry method.
5. 脳力＋ 支払い技術検定 (App Store JP) — a brain-training GAME about choosing optimal notes/coins to hand over. Strong cultural validation that 'what you hand over' is a real mental model; not a tracker, logs nothing.
6. D&D coin trackers (dndcoincalc.web.app, Nat20Tools, etc.) — increment/decrement gold/silver/copper inventory, even auto-make change; structurally similar inventory deltas but game tooling with no expense record and no price-derivation-as-entry.

Honest scoping the pitch should keep: the genuinely novel part is ONLY the handover keypad (entry by physical exchange, price derived). The 'pazar session' outing grouping is NOT novel (trip grouping exists in TravelSpend, Spendee shared wallets, Tricount), and denomination inventory per se is NOT novel (cash counters above). The whyNovel claim as written — 'no tracker on any platform has an entry method based on the handover of notes' — withstood 8 targeted searches across two languages and the three most plausible cash-economy app markets. Residual risk: an unindexed long-tail Android app in India/Indonesia could exist, but I cannot name one, and per the brief, unnamed prior art does not kill.

**Usefulness verdict:** VERDICT: survives — but two of its three headline claims die under the knife, and survival is conditional on positioning.

KILLED CLAIM 1 — "faster than typing digits." Honest tap accounting: round pazar prices are a tie or slight win (200 exact = 1 tap vs 3; 1300 via 2000 with 500+200 back = 3 vs 4), but any non-round price is a clear loss — 365 paid with 500 means tapping 100+20+10+5 change chips (5 taps) vs typing 3-6-5 (3 taps). Lek has 50/100/20/10/5 coins the pitch glosses over; the change row needs ~6 coin chips plus 5 notes. Net: tap-neutral in a round-price economy, not superior. The pitch overstates this.

KILLED CLAIM 2 — "you don't know you spent 350 lekë." Half false. At a stall the price is spoken aloud; it is usually the most salient fact. What people actually misremember is the exact CHANGE composition — which is the mandatory input here. A misremembered coin yields a silently wrong amount. If the user logs hours later, this method degrades BELOW the numpad.

WHAT ACTUALLY SAVES IT: (a) Zero-recall logging in the moment — at entry time the change is physically in your palm; you tap what you see, no number needs to have been registered. In a noisy market, one-handed with bags, that is a genuine recurring win, and Goldengo's shipped behavior (drop haptic, quick-log intents, ghost rows) shows this user logs immediately. (b) Frequency — it upgrades the single most common transaction type in an Albanian cash life, so even a marginal per-entry improvement compounds daily; this is an input-path redesign, not a destination feature, which is exactly the profile that survives week two. (c) Free data exhaust — every entry produces denomination in/out, which feeds The Count's reconciliation and shrinks wallet drift without any extra effort; this is the only entry method where logging makes reconciliation MORE accurate over time. (d) No homework, no maintenance, graceful failure — the numpad remains as fallback; cost of abandonment is zero.

CONDITIONS FOR SURVIVAL: (1) It must be the DEFAULT keypad for lek cash entries, not a mode behind a switch — a mode-switch tap erases the marginal advantage and habit will revert to the numpad by week three. (2) The derived amount must be displayed prominently ("= 350") as the sanity check against mis-tapped chips. (3) Its full value depends on The Count shipping; standalone it is merely tap-neutral-but-felt, which alone would be a soul piece, not a utility. Paired, it is the input half of a real reconciliation loop. (4) Soften the pitch's speed and psychology claims — the honest pitch is "no number needed, tap what's in your hand," not "faster than digits."

Not a demo: no setup, no one-time wow dependency (the animation is garnish, not the value), and it targets a pain (frictionful micro-cash entry plus wallet drift) that recurs multiple times daily for this specific user. The eight-stall pazar session is real unmet pain no listed competitor touches. It asks nothing and gives a little, many times a day — the opposite of the gimmick profile.

## Qerasja — generosity rendered as warmth, not loss

**Pitch:** When you log a coffee or a dinner, one optional tap marks it 'for someone' — Beni, Mom, the table. Those expenses carry a quiet ember tint in the ledger and a softer, doubled drop haptic: the money left, but it went somewhere warm. And when Beni pays for YOUR coffee, you tap your usual ghost row once and choose 'someone treated me' — a zero-lek entry that keeps your rhythm honest and remembers the warmth, with no debt, no balance, no one owing anyone anything. Month's end states it once, plainly: 'you treated 9 coffees; you were treated 6.'

**Why novel:** Every tracker treats generosity as a category called 'Gifts' — silently coded as leakage. Splitwise goes the other way and turns it into debt accounting. The unclaimed middle is generosity as a first-class emotional kind: spending-on-others rendered visually and haptically different from spending-on-self, plus the genuinely new zero-amount 'treated by' entry. No app distinguishes 'I didn't spend' from 'I was treated.' Deliberately anti-Splitwise: reciprocity is remembered, never tallied.

**Why useful:** In Albania, qerasja — treating the round — is not discretionary spending, it's social fabric, and one of the largest spend classes in a cash-culture life. Every existing tracker frames it as overspend, producing exactly the guilt that kills tracking. Daily utility is concrete: one extra tap at log time, and an honest Rhythm Ledger on treated days — today the day a friend pays for your usual coffee reads as a broken rhythm or a fabricated expense; this fixes a real truthfulness hole in a shipped system.

**Fit:** Deepens three shipped systems instead of competing with them: Rhythm Ledger (treated-by entries keep ghost rows truthful — extending the hard-won surface-ghosts-never-fabricate principle to zero-amount moments), the Weight (the math stays honest; only the felt rendering changes — ember tint, give-haptic), and Re-entry/recaps (given money is named as generosity, never as a leak). Anti-gamification holds absolutely: no generosity score, no giving streaks, no comparison — just memory of warmth.

**Build sketch:** One cycle, two small migrations. Extend ExpenseRecord (Sources/GoldengoData/Models/ExpenseRecord.swift) with givenKindRaw: String? (forSomeone / treatedBy) and personName: String? — string-typed people with a recents picker, no new PersonRecord model. 'Treated me' entries are amount 0 with givenKind = treatedBy, so FIFO Provenance allocation is untouched (zero draws nothing from sources). UI: one toggle-with-name-field in QuickAdd and the ghost-row swipe menu; an ember tint modifier on ledger rows; a CoreHaptics give-pattern variant beside the shipped drop haptic. The recap line is a deterministic count query. Decimal comparisons stay in memory post-fetch.

**Curator:** Won the sixth slot over Echo, Unbought, Patina, Open Tab, and The Weigh: it is the only contender that both fixes a data-truth hole in an already-shipped feature (treated days breaking Rhythm ghost rows) and fires at daily frequency in this user's culture. Echo and Unbought are the strongest cuts — soul-perfect but lower-frequency; The Weigh reads demo-ish against the 'daily life, not a demo' bar; Open Tab is platform novelty more than concept novelty. Shares the lightweight person substrate with Besa by design, without merging the two.

**Prior-art verdict:** SURVIVES — but only after a forced rewrite of its novelty claim, because I landed one real hit.

THE HIT: Tab Tracker (John Lein, iOS, v1.0 March 2025, App Store id6743378788) is a shipped standalone generosity log occupying exactly the "unclaimed middle" the concept claims for itself: you pick "the person you're treating" and what you treated them to (coffee, beer, a gift, a donation), view history by person or by item, with explicitly warm, anti-guilt framing ("a friendly reminder of your kindness") and zero debt/balance tallying. The whyNovel sentence "Every tracker treats generosity as a category called 'Gifts' — silently coded as leakage... The unclaimed middle is generosity as a first-class emotional kind" is therefore FALSE as written and must be cut or rewritten. Secondary partial prior art: Money Lover's "With" people-picker field on transactions (mainstream tracker, person-attached expenses, though semantically "who was present," not "this was for them," with no differential rendering); dedicated gift-per-person trackers (Gift Spending Tracker, GiftTracker); and Korean gyeongjosa/chugeuigeum gift-money apps that record social money given AND received per person — though those exist precisely to tally reciprocal obligation, the anti-pattern Qerasja disavows.

WHY IT STILL SURVIVES: I verified Tab Tracker's listing directly — it does NOT track being treated, has no zero-amount receiving entry, and is a parallel journal that never touches your real expense ledger. Despite targeted searches, I found no app anywhere with (a) a zero-amount "someone treated me" entry as a first-class record type in a real expense tracker (the only adjacent practice is the manual beancount/ledger idiom of booking a gifted meal as Income:Gifts offsetting Expenses:Dining — a power-user convention, not a product feature; official beancount docs only document the debt/receivables pattern), (b) differential felt rendering (tint + distinct haptic) of generosity inside one's primary ledger, or (c) a deliberately non-tallied two-way recap. Critically, the part with no prior art is the part that load-bears for Goldengo: the treated-by zero entry fixes a genuine truthfulness hole in the shipped Rhythm Ledger (a treated day currently reads as broken rhythm or forces a fabricated expense), which no standalone generosity journal can do.

NET: the giving-side emotional framing is prior art (Tab Tracker kills the broad rhetoric); the in-ledger integration, the zero-lek treated-by mechanic, and reciprocity-remembered-never-tallied survive as honest novelty. Condition for keeping the slot: narrow whyNovel to that bundle and name Tab Tracker as the near-miss. One internal nit: the month-end "you treated 9; you were treated 6" line IS a soft tally — phrase it carefully or it contradicts the anti-Splitwise stance.

Net: not a demo, not a novelty. One functional fix to a shipped system at daily cultural frequency, wrapped in on-brand ambience that costs nothing. The curator's claim — only contender that both fixes a data-truth hole and fires daily — checked out against the code.
